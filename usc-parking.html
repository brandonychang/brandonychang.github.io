01

One decision, scattered evidence

Interviews across active, hybrid, and rideshare commuters showed different travel habits but the same concerns: safety, time, crowding, and event-day uncertainty. Students lacked one place to compare travel modes, traffic conditions, alternate routes, parking, and crowd levels.

The product direction moved from general event planning toward transportation support. Xposition organized event-day choices around one question: what route fits this person’s timing, mobility needs, and tolerance for crowds?

From event to route

  3. 03

    Check crowdedness

    Translate the heatmap into crowd tolerance, density levels, forecasts, and alternate-route suggestions.

  4. 04

    Follow the complete route

    Connect the recommendation to directions, parking, live-location sharing, and suggested meeting points.

04

What usability testing changed

Interpretability

“Heatmap” became “crowdedness”

Participants understood the goal once density controls used human language and linked numbers to a visible crowd example.

Click clarity

Buttons needed boundaries

Stronger button segmentation, larger event pins, and fewer competing map markers clarified the next action.

Contrast

Routes needed visual separation

A grayed map, stronger route colors, and visible alternatives made meeting-point and commute choices easier to compare.

Completion

The flow needed an ending

Directions, a go button, friend controls, meeting recommendations, and alternate routes closed gaps in the first prototype.
