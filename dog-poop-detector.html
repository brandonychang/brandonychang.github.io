Dog waste changes shape, color, and visibility. A crouched spine, straight tail, and stationary pose form a more consistent signal.

01

From problem to observable signal

A generic object detector asks the camera to recognize waste after it appears. That approach breaks under grass, darkness, scale, occlusion, and changing appearance. The stronger product hypothesis recognizes the dog’s behavior at the moment the location is known.

The working definition combines three spine points, three tail points, body motion, and time. One frame never decides the event. Evidence accumulates across consecutive frames.

System notes

live.py

Video ingestion

Maintains the camera stream, samples frames, runs pose inference, passes coordinates to the processor, and handles reconnects.

DeepLabCut

Pose model

A transfer-learned model estimates the three spine and three tail points instead of requiring markers on the animal.

processor.py

Behavior logic

Computes spine curvature, tail alignment, stationarity, and temporal votes. Borderline frames remain candidates rather than immediate alerts.

event output

Map and evidence

Records timestamp and location, saves a screenshot or clip, and adds a marker to a locally served cleanup map.

04

What I would test next

Generalization

Different dogs and yards

Build a fixed evaluation set across breeds, coat colors, camera angles, backgrounds, and body proportions.

Failure modes

Stretching, sitting, digging

Save false alerts with keypoint overlays and feature logs, then add hard-negative examples to the next training cycle.

Robustness

Light and occlusion

Segment precision and recall by daylight, infrared, partial framing, moving objects, and keypoint confidence.

Productization

Privacy and edge deployment

Keep processing local, define retention controls, expose alert sensitivity, and separate ingestion, inference, detection, mapping, and notification modules.
