How Forkhead proteins help yeast copy DNA.

I built and checked mutant yeast strains to study how Forkhead proteins bind DNA and help selected replication origins start early.

2022–2025DNA Replication Research Assistant
Main question
How do Forkhead proteins connect a DNA sequence with early origin firing?
My project
Construct and verify an HCM1 DNA-binding mutant for controlled yeast experiments
Methods
PCR · yeast transformation · colony screening · sequencing · ChIP · FACS · qPCR

The project goal

Change one DNA-binding feature, then test what changes inside the cell.

Forkhead proteins connect DNA recognition with cell-cycle control. My work created a defined HCM1 mutation and a verified yeast strain so later experiments could compare the mutant with the original strain.

01Where does the protein bind?

DNA sequence and shape determine which sites a Forkhead domain recognizes.

02Which origins start early?

Fkh1 and Fkh2 help recruit limiting activation factors to selected origins.

03Did the edit cause the result?

PCR, selection, sequencing, and controls separate a true mutation from a failed experiment.

Core recognition

Helix H3 reads the main DNA sequence.

H3 sits in the DNA major groove and makes base-specific contacts with the GTAAACA core motif. This provides the main sequence-recognition step.

Main idea: the core motif tells Fkh1 where to bind.

Research context

Wang and colleagues reported a 2.2 Å Fkh1-DNA co-crystal structure. The structure showed a longer, stable Wing 2 and explained why R400 and R401 help read DNA beside the core motif.

Why the internal control mattered

A weak signal can come from biology or from the assay.

A known tagged protein and binding site provided a reference inside the same experiment. Comparing the experimental pull-down with this control helped separate a true binding change from poor antibody recovery, sample loss, or uneven library amplification.

What connected the work

Build a specific change, verify it, then measure its effect.

01

Forkhead proteins act before DNA copying begins

Fkh1 and Fkh2 bind selected origins in G1 and help those origins recruit limiting activation factors.

02

DNA outside the core motif matters

Fkh1 helix H3 reads the core sequence. Wing 1 and Wing 2 contact the flanking minor grooves and strengthen recognition.

03

Verification protects the conclusion

Selection finds candidates. Colony PCR checks location. Sequencing confirms the exact bases. Controls check assay quality.
