What Salve was working toward

Use computation to narrow the search for useful biological designs.

Salve Therapeutics was exploring computational tools for viral biologics, including treatments aimed at rare diseases. One costly question in that process is whether a candidate molecule is likely to bind to the intended protein target strongly enough to deserve more testing.

01The project goal

Create a repeatable way to estimate protein–ligand binding affinity from PDB structure files, so candidate designs could be compared before laboratory screening.

02My contribution

Parse protein and ligand structures, encode their features, join them with measured affinity values, generate controlled amino-acid substitutions, and train baseline neural networks.

03What the work produced

A reusable PDB-to-model dataset pipeline, modified structure files for future experiments, and a measured baseline that showed both the model’s promise and where it failed to generalize.

05 / What I built

Parsing, encoding, mutation, and reconstruction.

  • Extracted amino-acid sequences from PDB structures
  • Detected ligand heteroatoms and encoded common atom types
  • Generated chemically similar residue substitutions at a chosen position
  • Rebuilt modified protein and ligand chains into new PDB files
  • Joined encoded inputs with binding-affinity labels for training

What the experiment showed

The pipeline worked; the first model was a baseline, not a finished predictor.

The project delivered a reusable way to turn PDB structures into model-ready records and compare architectures. The training curves also exposed the main limitation: after the early epochs, training error kept falling while performance on unseen complexes became worse. That made early stopping, stronger structural features, and stricter validation the next concrete priorities.
