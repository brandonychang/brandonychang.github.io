01 / Why the inbox mattered

A familiar front door.
A hidden workload.

Field teams already knew where to go for help: email the analytics team. That made the inbox useful, but it also made it a gathering place for very different kinds of work. A request to open a report, a question about a number, and a broken data source could all arrive through the same channel.

The operational cost extended beyond answering email. When people cannot access a tool or trust a report, their work stalls while analysts investigate. The goal was to reduce that friction: get each request to the right person, keep it moving, and use the patterns to prevent repeat problems.

Access & permissions

Help people reach the tools.

Access requests were the largest category. Their frequency pointed to an opportunity to improve access guidance and routing at the source.

Data validation & debugging

Help people trust the answer.

A question about a number can require root-cause analysis: reproduce the discrepancy, trace the source data and report logic, check definitions, and debug the issue before validating the result. These investigations take sustained analyst time; counting emails alone does not capture that effort.

Reporting & technical support

Keep field operations moving.

Report questions and issues across tools and data sources need context, follow-through, and sometimes another team’s help. A shared record makes the owner and next action visible while that work continues.

My role & product goal

Keep email simple. Make the work behind it accountable.

I built the Outlook-to-Python pipeline, classification and priority rules, conversation deduplication, owner assignment, service-level tracking, and dashboard. The team continued using the same inbox; automation added the shared record and follow-up around it.

For the field
A familiar way to ask for help, with less uncertainty about who is handling the request.
For the analytics team
One queue to assign, prioritize, update, and hand off work across five teammates.
For leadership
Evidence for staffing and priority decisions: how much support work arrives, where it accumulates, and which recurring issues deserve investment.

02 / Before & after

From remembering requests
to managing a queue.

Before / Manual follow-up

An email was the record.

  • Requests also surfaced in Teams, meetings, and conversations, without a shared backlog to reconcile them.
  • Ownership, priority, due dates, and status depended on people remembering to follow up.
  • Leaders heard about the workload but could not reliably size it or see aging work.

After / Structured intake

Each thread has a place.

  • Inbox requests are captured automatically; conversation deduplication keeps replies on one record.
  • Each item carries an owner, category, priority, status, and a service-level deadline.
  • A daily dashboard and Monday morning digest surface outstanding work and overdue items.

How it works

Automate coordination.
Give investigation room.

  1. 01

    Capture

    Read the shared inbox and Sent Items; group messages by conversation.

  2. 02

    Classify

    Structure application, workstream, request type, and priority.

  3. 03

    Assign

    Route to an owner and set a service level, from one day for urgent work to ten for routine work.

  4. 04

    Follow through

    Use reply keywords to close, reassign, or reprioritize requests. Preserve manual overrides.

  5. 05

    Make visible

    Refresh the dashboard daily and send outstanding items to owners every Monday at 7 a.m.

03 / What changed

A basis for decisions,
not just a cleaner inbox.

Demand became measurable

69 in July. 75 in August.

By summer 2026, monthly intake had grown well beyond the roughly 6–20 requests seen at launch. Leaders responsible for staffing and priorities could now see support as a recurring workstream and discuss capacity with evidence.

Coordination became lighter

More time for the actual problem.

Automatic capture, routing, and reminders reduced manual administration around each request. The tracker reports an average completion time of about 1.3 days; complex validation and debugging cases can take longer. There is no measured pre-tracker speed baseline for comparison.

Risk became visible

40 open. 27 marked overdue.

The oldest recorded open request was 150 days old. Surfacing aging work helps leaders decide where to unblock a dependency or add attention. These are recorded statuses: missed closure signals can leave completed work looking open.

Estimated capacity returned

About 84–126 hours of coordination.

502 requests × an assumed 10–15 minutes of manual sorting, logging, and follow-up per request. This is an estimate, not a measured time study, and covers coordination rather than the investigation itself. Measuring the actual time removed is a next step.

04 / Lessons & next steps

The queue showed us
what to improve next.

01 / Prevent repeat demand

Use the categories to fix recurring friction.

Access led request volume, while the tracker also surfaced ten technical issues across tools and data sources. The opportunity is to improve access guidance and investigate recurring failures together. Next, distinguish high-volume requests from high-effort investigations so priority reflects both frequency and field impact.

02 / Make status trustworthy

Capture the resolution, wherever the reply happens.

Replies from personal mailboxes do not always reach the tracker, and vague replies can leave items in progress. Clear closure markers and broader owner-reply capture are on the fix list. Reconciling these gaps matters before treating the overdue count as confirmed unresolved work.

03 / Design for daily habits

Bring the next action to the team.

Teammates asked for the Monday digest because it reminded them without requiring a dashboard visit. That feedback became a shipped feature. Eighteen captured feedback items, including fifteen improvement ideas, now provide a backlog for continued iteration.

04 / Build for continuity

Move beyond a prototype on one machine.

Python and Excel proved the workflow without a new platform, license, or headcount. The next decision is whether to move to a more durable setup, such as a SharePoint List, Power BI, and event-driven capture, and whether to extend it beyond Data & Analytics. That is a proposed direction, not a completed migration.

My biggest lesson: a tracker creates value when its data changes what the team does next—who takes a request, which problem gets fixed upstream, and where leaders invest so field teams can keep working.

Source: HSG Inbox Management dashboard and tracker, summarized September 14, 2026. Figures describe the recorded workload; coordination savings are estimated. First response and completion measure different stages of a request.
